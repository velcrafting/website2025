## Discovery questions

- What are the audience's recurring questions, misconceptions or unmet information needs?
- Which facts have authoritative sources, and who can verify them?
- What information is public, client-confidential, commercially restricted or internal-only?
- Which channels and formats are actually used by the audience?
- Who owns factual review, publication, escalation and later maintenance?
- What examples show the current problem? Which previous responses worked or failed?
- How will usefulness be evaluated without assuming reach, trust or citation outcomes?

## Verification and handoff

Review factual accuracy, audience comprehension, accessibility, source currency and consistency across the agreed material. Check that confidential information and unsupported claims have not entered the publication candidate. Record factual corrections and version changes.

If publication is included, capture authorization for the exact version and destination. Preparing a draft does not complete publication. Acknowledging receipt does not establish approval of its content.

## Risks and change triggers

Revisit scope when product facts change, a new audience or channel is added, source reliability is disputed, a live incident replaces planned work, or continuing monitoring is requested. Record the proposed change, urgency, responsible decision-maker and effect on deliverables. Preserve the accepted baseline and earlier published versions.
